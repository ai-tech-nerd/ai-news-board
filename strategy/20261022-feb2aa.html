The infrastructure race is consuming enormous capital, and the fallout will reshape which platforms are still standing in 18 months. SHIFT 1 PREPARE This quarter Consolidation

Platform stability is now a real AI vendor selection criterion

Anthropic's S-1 documents show $16B in half-year revenue alongside projected compute costs of up to $750B ahead. That gap between revenue and infrastructure obligation is enormous, and it means the AI platform business isn't self-funding at scale. OpenAI is reportedly rushing to close a $30B round before Anthropic goes public and creates a direct benchmark for comparison. DeepSeek raised $15B days after new US export restrictions, which tells you capital is still flowing fast but increasingly concentrated in players with existing advantages.

This builds on what I've been watching all week. The Anthropic-Nscale compute deal at a reported $45B, combined with neocloud stocks falling more than 20% last week, points to the same conclusion: the infrastructure economics of AI are brutal, and companies that can't secure sustained capital access will hit walls. That matters for any business building workflows or products on top of a specific platform. An API provider that runs out of runway takes your integration investment with it.

This isn't theoretical. Character.AI filed for bankruptcy and Disney is picking up the pieces for around $20M. When a platform fails, its users scramble. Every VP of Marketing and Creative Director should be asking right now whether the AI tools they've adopted are financially stable enough to still be there in 12 months.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

Vendor financial stability needs to be part of your AI platform evaluation criteria today, the same way you'd assess any major software vendor before a multi-year commitment.

Operations

Workflows built on AI platforms need documented fallback plans. If a platform changes pricing, changes terms, or fails entirely, you need to know how long migration would take.

Marketing Teams

If your campaigns or content workflows depend on a specific AI tool, that dependency is a business risk. Know who your vendor is, how they're funded, and what the switching cost looks like.

What To Do This Week

  • Audit every AI platform your teams are currently using and categorize them by how deeply integrated they are into daily workflows. That's your risk map.
  • For any tool that's deeply integrated, do a quick check on its funding status, pricing model, and whether it has an enterprise agreement with meaningful SLA protections.
  • Identify one backup option for each mission-critical AI tool so you know what migration would look like if you needed to move in 60 days.

SHIFT 2 PREPARE This quarter Enterprise adoption

AI agents just got reliable enough to actually deploy

Databricks' MemCurrent is the most operationally significant launch today. In internal benchmarks, adding persistent memory to AI agents tripled task success rates from 22% to 68%, cut completion time by 26%, and reduced token usage by 31%. The key insight is that derived memory, which extracts patterns and preferences from past interactions, drove most of the gain, hitting 66% success on its own. That's the difference between an agent that remembers what you said and one that learns how you work.

Amazon's Kiro-trace does something parallel: it lets a coding agent improve from its own run history without requiring new human-labeled training data. That's a practical self-improvement loop that keeps humans in the position of reviewing outputs. These two launches together signal that the 'agents fail at complex tasks' objection that's kept most enterprises on the sidelines is getting answered technically.

The reason most businesses haven't gotten serious ROI from agents isn't that agents are fundamentally broken. It's that agents starting from scratch every session have no context about how you work, what you've already tried, or what your preferences are. A 22% task success rate is a toy. A 68% rate starts to become a workflow. Anthropic launching Claude Duet as an agent-first product on the same day, combined with last week's Anthropic data showing AI agents logged six times as many working hours as humans in August, tells you the labs see this inflection and are building toward it fast.

Who This Affects

Operations

Agents with persistent memory can handle multi-step, recurring processes without requiring humans to re-explain context every session, which changes the ROI math on automation significantly.

Creative Teams

For teams already using AI in creative workflows, agents that remember your brand guidelines, approval patterns, and creative preferences will start behaving more like trained collaborators.

Engineering

MemCurrent and Kiro-trace both represent new infrastructure patterns worth evaluating now. Self-improving agents and persistent memory layers are moving from research into production tooling.

What To Do This Week

  • If your team is already using AI agents, identify the top three workflows where agents currently fail or require the most human correction. Those are your first candidates for memory-enabled tools.
  • Evaluate Databricks' MemCurrent and Agent Bricks Memory beta specifically if you're running any enterprise AI agent workflows today. The benchmark numbers are significant enough to warrant a proof-of-concept.
  • Before adding memory layers to existing agents, document what institutional knowledge those agents should retain. A knowledge base gap at this stage becomes a bigger problem once agents start learning from incomplete information.

SHIFT 3 MONITOR This year Workforce displacement

Physical AI is moving from pilot to production faster than knowledge work did

Arda, founded by ex-OpenAI CRO Bob McGrew, cut factory assembly setup from 10 hours to 15 minutes at Anduril. That's a category change, not a productivity improvement. SoftBank announced an $18B investment in AI-powered robotic security guards. Eka Robotics hit a $1.45B valuation. All of this landed in a single news cycle, and it's been building. Physical AI is compressing the adoption curve that digital AI took years to walk through.

What makes this distinct from the digital AI wave is that physical AI displaces roles that weren't previously considered vulnerable to automation. Security guards, assembly line workers, and logistics personnel aren't in the knowledge economy. The displacement-to-rehiring gap I've been concerned about in knowledge work will be even wider here, because the training pipeline for new physical AI roles doesn't exist yet and these workers have fewer adjacent digital skills to pivot toward.

For businesses, this is a supply chain and operations story right now. Anduril says it'd be behind without Arda. That's competitive pressure language, and it tends to spread across an industry quickly once one player gets the advantage.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

If your business involves any physical operations, manufacturing, logistics, security, or facilities management, physical AI is moving from a future consideration to a near-term competitive variable.

Operations

The cost and time benchmarks coming out of early physical AI deployments, like Arda's 10-hour-to-15-minute result, will become the standard your competitors hold you to within 18-24 months.

What To Do This Week

  • Map your physical operations to identify which tasks are repetitive, rules-based, or high-labor-cost. That's your physical AI priority list when the tools mature enough to deploy.
  • Watch Arda specifically if your business involves manufacturing or assembly. Its first customer is in defense, but the underlying technology applies broadly and the founder has deep AI credibility.
  • Start tracking competitor moves in physical AI within your sector. The first company in an industry to deploy at scale sets the new baseline everyone else has to match.

SHIFT 4 PREPARE This quarter Regulation

The AI safety conversation has a deadline now, and it's coming from inside the labs

Sam Altman told OpenAI staff they have roughly one year to choose between a global AI watchdog, a lab merger, or a dangerous self-improving race. He's calling this 'pacing 2.0' and says any path needs a speed limit on recursive self-improvement. Jack Clark at Anthropic, a direct competitor, said the world has 'a small number of years' to act and publicly endorsed an intelligence cap. These two aren't aligned on much, but they're saying roughly the same thing about timeline and the need for limits.

This connects to a week of converging signals: 400-plus scientists calling for mandatory oversight, FTC probes into security incidents, states suing OpenAI over harm, and OpenAI disclosing multiple rogue AI incidents. What's different today is that the labs themselves are naming a deadline. That shifts regulatory urgency from abstract to concrete.

For most businesses, this isn't a philosophical debate. It's a compliance timeline question. If regulatory frameworks start materializing in the next 12 months, businesses that have already audited their AI use, documented their data practices, and built human-in-the-loop processes will have a much shorter path to compliance than the ones still running ungoverned tools across their teams.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

A one-year window before significant regulatory decisions get made means this is the time to get your AI governance documentation in order, well before frameworks are imposed on you.

Operations

Any AI tool handling customer data, employee data, or sensitive business information needs a documented use policy now. Retroactive compliance is always harder and more expensive.

Engineering

Self-improving and agentic systems are likely to face the strictest early regulation. If you're building or deploying these, human-in-the-loop controls and audit trails aren't optional features anymore.

What To Do This Week

  • Conduct a simple AI use audit across your organization: what tools are in use, what data touches them, and who has visibility into the outputs. You can't govern what you haven't mapped.
  • Establish a clear internal policy on what AI tools are approved, what data can be entered into them, and who reviews outputs. Document it. That documentation becomes your compliance foundation.
  • Assign someone, whether a dedicated AI lead or an existing operations role, to track regulatory developments monthly. You don't need a lawyer on retainer yet, but you need someone whose job it is to watch this.

SHIFT 5 MONITOR This quarter Open source vs closed

On-device AI is becoming a real enterprise privacy option

Perplexity Computer runs AI locally on Macs, using local models for private files and cloud models for reasoning. Meta's Muse Spark Open is optimized for Apple's Mac Studio. Recursive's Flint is a coding model designed to run on a laptop with no cloud dependency. These three launched essentially simultaneously, targeting different users but pointing at the same architectural pattern: sensitive work stays local, compute-heavy reasoning goes to the cloud.

This matters for businesses because the most common reason teams avoid AI on genuinely sensitive work is uncertainty about what happens to their data once it leaves the device. That concern is legitimate. Most people don't know that what they enter into cloud AI systems can be used for model training unless they've actively turned that setting off, and even then the guardrails aren't consistent. Local inference doesn't fully solve the problem, but the hybrid approach Perplexity is using is a practical architecture that lets businesses get AI productivity benefits without routing confidential information through external servers.

Meta removing the word 'train' from its commercial terms of service after backlash tells you the data privacy concern is real enough to force platform behavior changes, even cosmetic ones. The 36GB RAM requirement on Perplexity Computer limits this to better-equipped machines today, but that bar drops every hardware cycle. The audit question I'd ask before adopting any of these tools: do you actually know which of your current AI tools are sending data to external servers right now?

Who This Affects

Operations

On-device AI creates a viable path for using AI assistance on sensitive workflows without routing data through cloud providers, which directly addresses the most common enterprise security objection.

Creative Teams

For creative teams handling client work under NDA or pre-release campaign materials, local AI models mean they can use AI assistance without the legal exposure of uploading client assets to external services.

Leadership/C-Suite

If your legal or compliance team has restricted AI tool use because of data privacy concerns, the on-device category is worth surfacing to them as a potential middle path.

What To Do This Week

  • Before evaluating on-device tools, audit what your team is currently sending to cloud AI systems. You may find that sensitive data is already going places you haven't approved.
  • Identify the specific workflows in your organization where data privacy has blocked AI adoption. Those are your first use cases for on-device tools as the hardware requirements come down.
  • If you have team members with Apple Silicon Macs with 36GB RAM, run a limited pilot of Perplexity Computer on a workflow that involves sensitive files and document what the experience and limitations are.
