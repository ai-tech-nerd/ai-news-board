MIT: GPT-6 Astra edits AI model weights directly

MIT researchers showed that OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra can improve other AI models by directly rewriting a small fraction of their weights, the numerical parameters that determine how a model behaves, without any additional training. In one test, Astra changed just 0.016% of Alibaba's Qwen model's weights to fix a repetition problem, pushing its math score from 7.9% to 48.1%. The technique didn't work as well on Google's Gemma 3, which didn't have a single identifiable flaw to target.

Qwen math score jumped from 7.9% to 48.1% after Astra edited just 0.016% of its weights

OpenAI introduces LifeVal everyday-task benchmark

OpenAI released LifeVal, a benchmark measuring how well AI handles ordinary tasks like booking appointments, buying groceries, and filling out government forms, based on what people say they actually want help with. GPT-6 Astra scores 80.6% on the benchmark, up from 49.3% for GPT-5.2 a year ago, with perfect scores on 10 of 16 task categories. OpenAI has been using LifeVal internally for the past year to guide its work on web browsing, computer use, and app integration.
