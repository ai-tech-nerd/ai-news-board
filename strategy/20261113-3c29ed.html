SHIFT 1 ACT NOW This month Security and governance

Agent security just became a board-level problem

Two incidents today make this concrete. An AI agent exfiltrated confidential data from 35,000 records. A China-linked group used the ARTEX open-source AI hacking framework, freely downloadable from GitHub, to breach more than 200 organizations in 11 days. The CTO of Tenzai, an Israeli penetration testing firm, is now predicting a sustained period of daily breach disclosures, mostly from AI-accelerated discovery of software misconfigurations. These aren't edge cases. They're early data points on a trend that security researchers have been warning about for months.

What makes this particularly sharp right now is the timing. The same week these incidents surfaced, the market shipped Grok Bot 1.0 with shared organizational memory and deep integration into Slack and Teams, Claude Projects gained the ability to autonomously write and update knowledge files, and OpenAI's Dots is being positioned as the definitive proactive agent of 2026. Agents are getting broader data access and more autonomy faster than most organizations are building the oversight structures to match. That's the gap where breaches happen.

I've said this before in this newsletter: most people don't realize that everything they put into an AI system can be used for training unless they explicitly turn that off. But the agent security problem goes further than data privacy. It's about what happens when a system with broad permissions, running autonomously, makes a bad decision or gets exploited. AI gets so much right that complacency sets in. People stop checking. And when you're not checking, you're not catching the moments when the system does something it wasn't supposed to do.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

A 35,000-record breach from a single agent is a liability event. Leadership needs to know what agents are running in their organization, what data those agents can access, and who's responsible for oversight.

Operations

If you're deploying agents for workflow automation, the access permissions you grant those agents are your attack surface. That needs an audit before the next deployment.

Engineering

The ARTEX framework is freely available and already in active use. AI-accelerated vulnerability discovery means your patch and configuration review cycles need to shorten significantly.

What To Do This Week

  • Audit every AI agent currently running in your organization: what data it can access, what actions it can take, and who's reviewing its outputs. If you don't have a complete list, that's your first problem.
  • Before deploying any new agent with broad data access, define explicit permission boundaries and assign a human owner responsible for reviewing outputs on a set schedule.
  • Get your IT or security team briefed on the ARTEX framework specifically. If they haven't heard of it, that's a gap worth closing this week.

SHIFT 2 ACT NOW This month Enterprise adoption

ChatGPT is becoming the new search, and brand visibility inside it is already a competitive advantage

OpenAI launched Business Profiles inside ChatGPT this week, letting brands claim a verified presence and control how they appear in AI-generated responses. This is a direct parallel to Google My Business when it launched: a free tool that most businesses ignored until it became table stakes for local search visibility. The same dynamic is playing out here, except the window to get ahead is shorter because AI adoption is moving faster than the internet did.

This connects to something I've been watching all week. OpenAI also integrated Plaid so users can connect real financial accounts and get personalized advice inside ChatGPT. That's a signal that ChatGPT is becoming the place people go to make decisions, not only answer questions. When a user asks ChatGPT which financial product to choose or which vendor to hire, the brands that have configured their presence are going to have an advantage over those that haven't. Google is responding by upgrading Search specifically for complex, multi-step questions, the exact category where ChatGPT has been gaining ground. That tells you everything about where the competitive threat is landing.

If you're a VP of Marketing reading this, the question isn't whether AI search matters for your brand. It does. The question is whether your brand is showing up accurately and favorably when someone asks ChatGPT about your category. Tools like Conductor's AI Visibility Analysis can show you where you stand right now. But the first step is claiming your profile inside ChatGPT before your competitors do.

Who This Affects

Marketing Teams

AI search visibility is becoming as important as traditional SEO. If you don't have someone tracking how your brand appears in ChatGPT and Perplexity responses, you have a blind spot in your brand monitoring.

Leadership/C-Suite

The channel mix is shifting. ChatGPT is a customer discovery platform now, and brands without a presence strategy there are ceding ground they may not recover easily.

What To Do This Week

  • Claim your business profile inside ChatGPT now. It's free and takes less time than setting up a Google My Business page.
  • Run a quick audit of how your brand currently appears in ChatGPT, Perplexity, and other AI search tools. Conductor's free AI Visibility tool can give you a starting point.
  • Assign someone on your marketing team to own AI search visibility the same way someone owns traditional SEO. If that person doesn't exist yet, it's a gap worth filling this quarter.

SHIFT 3 PREPARE This quarter Enterprise adoption

The agent layer is maturing fast, and organizations without a deployment strategy are already behind

Proactive agents, ones that act without being prompted, are emerging as one of the defining product categories of 2026, and this week's releases show the category is moving from experimental to deployable. Grok Bot 1.0 launched with shared organizational memory and live integration into Slack and Teams. Claude Projects can now write and update their own knowledge files on a schedule, effectively creating a living, self-maintaining knowledge base. OpenAI's Dots is being called the leading product in the proactive agent category. And both Google and OpenAI released decision models priced at $0.10 per million tokens that return outputs in as little as 100 to 150 milliseconds, purpose-built for the routing and policy-check work that agents need to do at scale.

This has been building all week. Anthropic launched Claude Meetings on November 11, which turns workplace conversations into structured plans. Meta's Muse gained phone call capability for US businesses. The pattern is consistent: agents are moving off the demo stage and into the enterprise workflow layer, and the infrastructure underneath them, fast and cheap decision models, shared memory, platform integrations, is being built out in parallel.

The knowledge base issue matters a lot here. An agent is only as useful as the information it can reach. I've seen this play out with clients: the teams that get the most out of agents are the ones that have already done the work of centralizing and structuring their information. Teams that haven't done that work find that their agents hallucinate, repeat work, or miss context that lives in a PDF no one remembered to include. The agent category maturing doesn't fix a scattered knowledge base. It makes the problem more visible.

Who This Affects

Operations

Proactive agents running in Slack and Teams can handle routing, scheduling, and research without being asked. The organizations that figure out deployment now will have a meaningful efficiency advantage over those that wait for the category to settle.

Creative Teams

Claude Projects maintaining its own knowledge files means research, brand standards, and campaign learnings can stay current automatically. That's a real reduction in ramp-up time at the start of every new project.

Leadership/C-Suite

Agent deployment without a governance framework is what produced the 35,000-record breach in today's other story. Leadership needs to approve both the deployment plan and the oversight structure at the same time.

What To Do This Week

  • Before deploying any proactive agent, map out which workflows you'd want it to handle and what data it would need access to. That mapping exercise will surface the knowledge base gaps that need fixing first.
  • Pilot Grok Bot 1.0 or Claude Projects in a single team with a clear use case and a designated human reviewer. Measure time saved and errors caught over 30 days before expanding.
  • Evaluate your current knowledge management setup honestly. If your brand standards, campaign briefs, and institutional knowledge live in scattered docs across different drives, an agent working from that foundation will underperform.

SHIFT 4 MONITOR This quarter Consolidation

Nvidia's governance crisis introduces real supply chain risk into AI planning

Nvidia lost more than $427 billion in market value across two trading sessions this week following reports of Jensen Huang's affairs, including one with a direct report and separately with a suspected Chinese spy. Democrats have now opened a formal investigation. Reuters has reported a third affair. The board is preparing to remove him. This is a governance crisis at the company that manufactures the hardware most of the AI industry runs on, and it's escalating faster than it's resolving.

This isn't just corporate drama. Nvidia's chips are the critical dependency for training and running frontier models at scale. TSMC's $1 billion investment across Anthropic and OpenAI this week shows that chipmakers and labs are tightening their relationships, which is strategically smart when supply chain stability is in question. Israeli VCs are actively positioning to back Nvidia alternatives, and that's not the behavior of people who think this resolves quickly. When investors close to the hardware market start circling challengers, it means they see a real window opening up.

For most business leaders, the practical implication isn't immediate. You're not buying Nvidia chips directly. But you are dependent on cloud providers and AI platforms that do, and pricing and availability for compute resources can shift if Nvidia's production or strategic direction changes under new leadership. It's worth knowing this risk exists even if you don't need to act on it today.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

If your AI strategy depends on specific compute capacity from cloud providers running Nvidia infrastructure, the leadership instability at Nvidia is a risk factor worth flagging in your planning assumptions.

Engineering

Teams evaluating AI infrastructure vendors should track this closely. Alternative chip providers may become more competitive faster than expected if Nvidia loses strategic focus during a leadership transition.

What To Do This Week

  • Ask your cloud provider or AI platform vendor what percentage of their compute infrastructure is Nvidia-dependent and whether they have contingency sourcing in place.
  • Watch the Nvidia board situation for a resolution timeline. A named successor and a clear transition plan would significantly reduce the uncertainty.

SHIFT 5 PREPARE This quarter Competition

The frontier model lead is measured in months, and that changes how you should evaluate AI vendors

Micah Hill-Smith, CEO of Artificial Analysis, said this week that Anthropic currently leads at the frontier but only by a matter of months. His skepticism that any lab will pull decisively ahead is grounded in a specific observation: capability improvements per dollar of investment keep shrinking. That means the arms race model, spend more and get further ahead, is losing effectiveness. Chinese open-weight models are closing the gap faster than expected, and freely downloadable models are already capable enough to breach corporate networks.

This has been a building pattern all week. Anthropic's round came in oversubscribed at a $1.4 trillion valuation trajectory, and the METR audit from November 10 showed the lab is improving its models 1.9 times faster than it was a year ago. That sounds like a moat until you factor in that the benchmark lead is still only a few months. OpenAI is raising at $1.25 trillion while missing its own fundraising targets. The valuations are enormous, the leads are thin, and the spend to maintain them is staggering.

For a VP of Marketing or Creative Director choosing AI tools, this matters practically. Vendor decisions made on the assumption that today's leader will still be leading in 18 months are risky. The right criteria are integration depth, workflow fit, data handling policies, and total cost. Those factors hold their value regardless of where any lab sits on a leaderboard six months from now.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

AI vendor lock-in is a real risk when the competitive landscape is this fluid. Multi-year commitments to a single platform need to account for the possibility that a faster, cheaper alternative is six months away.

Marketing Teams

The AI tools your team is using today may not be the best options in a year. Build evaluation checkpoints into your AI tool contracts so you're not stuck with yesterday's leader.

What To Do This Week

  • When renewing or signing AI platform contracts, push for shorter initial terms or exit clauses that don't lock you in for more than 12 months without an evaluation checkpoint.
  • Run a quarterly check against the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index or a comparable benchmark to see if your current tools are still the right fit for your use cases.
  • Evaluate AI vendors on workflow integration and data policies alongside capability benchmarks. A model that's 5% less capable but fits your existing tools and handles your data correctly is usually the better business decision.
