Jensen Huang is back at Nvidia after 11 days. Anthropic just completed the biggest IPO in history. Claude helped prove a 167-year-old math problem. The signal is that AI capability just crossed a threshold serious people can't walk back from. When Meta's AI chief declares superintelligence is arriving and Scale AI's CEO calls the Riemann proof the hardest intellectual achievement humanity has ever produced, those aren't marketing statements. They're people going on record because they feel they have to.

What I'd focus on is the infrastructure war heating up underneath all of this. Amazon paid $7.5 billion for Decart, betting that smarter software can close its chip gap with Nvidia. And Google just removed the search button from its homepage and gave AI Mode access to your Drive and Wallet files. These aren't incremental product updates. The platforms your teams use every day are being rebuilt around AI as the primary layer.

For a VP of Marketing or a Creative Director trying to figure out where to focus right now, here's the honest answer: the external chaos doesn't change your immediate job, but it does change the urgency. The AI supply chain leakage story today, where a researcher planted 2 million canaries across AI pipelines and found widespread data leakage, is a direct problem for any company that hasn't audited what data its people are feeding into AI tools. That's most companies. The Notion co-founder's org-wide AI context guide hitting 6.5 million views before the weekend ended tells you that the knowledge-base problem I've been writing about for months just became mainstream. If you haven't started building a shared context layer for your organization, you're behind.

Strategic Shifts · Nov 24

SHIFT 1 ACT NOW This quarter Capability acceleration

AI capability just crossed a line that changes the risk calculus for businesses sitting on the sidelines

The Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis isn't a party trick. It's 17 million lines of machine-verifiable Lean code solving a problem that stumped every mathematician for 167 years, and it's been certified correct by independent communities. The immediate response from Meta's AI chief and Alexandr Wang at Scale AI wasn't hedged or qualified. They went on record calling this the beginning of superintelligence. That's a significant move from people who've been careful with their words.

This connects directly to what's been building all week. On November 19th, GPT-6.2 Astra became the first publicly released model to hit the 'High' threshold for AI self-improvement under OpenAI's own Preparedness Framework. The day before, OpenAI's chief scientist publicly called for limits on AI development pace. These aren't coincidental. The people building these systems are watching capability milestones arrive faster than expected, and their public statements are starting to reflect that.

For business leaders, the practical implication is straightforward: the gap between what AI can do and what most organizations think it can do just widened again. I catch myself doing this too, assuming AI can't handle something and then finding out it can. If that's happening to people on the front lines, the lag for everyone else is substantial. Waiting for the technology to mature before acting isn't a safe position when the technology just solved a 167-year-old mathematical conjecture.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

If your AI strategy is still in planning mode, today's milestone and the public declarations from industry leaders should accelerate that timeline. The question isn't whether AI will affect your business; it's whether you'll have infrastructure in place when it does.

Marketing Teams

AI reasoning at this level means content research, competitive analysis, and strategic synthesis tasks you currently assign to analysts or agency partners are increasingly automatable. The smart move is figuring out which of those tasks you want to own differently.

What To Do This Week

  • If you haven't done an AI Opportunity Assessment for your department, start there. You need a baseline of where the most friction is before you can know where capability jumps like this one actually matter to your workflow.
  • Brief your leadership team on the Riemann milestone in plain terms: AI reasoning capability is ahead of where most business planning assumes it is, and that gap is widening.
  • Review your current AI tool stack against what's now possible. Tools you evaluated six months ago may have capability gaps that no longer exist.

SHIFT 2 ACT NOW This month Enterprise platform consolidation

The platforms you already pay for are quietly becoming AI-first, and your team's data is being pulled in

Google removing the search button from its homepage is the most visible sign yet that AI-first search is now the default. The more important move is what's happening underneath: Google's AI Mode can now access your personal Drive files and Wallet data to personalize responses. That's a fundamental change in what search actually is. It's no longer a web index. It's a personal assistant with access to your documents.

This pattern has been building all week. On November 20th, Microsoft declared Copilot the 'new OS for work' at Ignite, with Office apps now running inside Copilot. On November 18th, we noted that Microsoft and Google hold a structural agent edge because they already own the apps, the data, and the user trust. Today's Google Search redesign and Drive integration confirms that edge is being cashed in. These companies don't need you to adopt a new AI tool. They're embedding AI into the tools you already can't stop using.

Here's what that means practically: your employees are probably already using AI Mode in Search without realizing their Drive documents are now part of the query context. That's a data governance issue most IT and legal teams haven't caught up to. And for Marketing and Creative teams, it means the tools in your existing stack are getting more capable whether you've planned for it or not. The audit question has expanded. It's no longer just 'what AI tools are we buying?' It's 'what AI capabilities have been quietly switched on in the tools we already have?'

Who This Affects

Operations

Drive and Wallet integration in Google Search means employee documents may now surface in AI-generated responses in ways that weren't possible before. Data governance policies need to account for this, and most don't yet.

Marketing Teams

Google's AI-first search redesign changes how content gets found. If your SEO strategy is built around traditional keyword and link signals, the shift to conversational AI Mode as the primary interface means that strategy needs a rethink.

Leadership/C-Suite

Microsoft and Google are embedding AI into the platforms your company already depends on. The question has moved past whether to adopt AI. The question now is whether you have visibility into what's already running.

What To Do This Week

  • Audit which Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 AI features are currently active across your organization. Features like Drive integration in Search AI Mode may be on by default, and your team is likely using them without realizing it.
  • Talk to your IT and legal team specifically about the Drive and Wallet integration in Google Search AI Mode. Understand what data is accessible, to whom, and under what conditions before it becomes a compliance problem.
  • Review your content strategy for AI Mode search. The traditional click-based discovery model is being replaced by conversational retrieval, which rewards different kinds of content structure and authority.

SHIFT 3 ACT NOW This month Security and governance

The AI supply chain has a data leakage problem most businesses don't know they're inside

A researcher planted 2 million canary tokens across the AI supply chain and found widespread leakage of private user data throughout AI pipelines. This wasn't a targeted breach. It was a systematic experiment that revealed sensitive information moving through third-party AI infrastructure in ways most users and companies have no visibility into. That's a materially different kind of problem than a one-off hack. It means the pipes themselves are leaky.

This lands on top of an already troubling week for AI security. On November 20th, Meta's Muse agent was found to be mixing up conversations between users and potentially exposing private data across accounts. The day before, Google's Threat Intelligence Group reported that AI-powered cyber campaigns are growing more sophisticated faster than defenses can respond. And today, Amazon reported that AI agents autonomously found and exploited a zero-day vulnerability with minimal human involvement across the full attack chain, including discovery, analysis, and exploitation.

Most businesses I talk to have no idea that everything their employees enter into AI systems can be used for training unless they've specifically turned that off, and even then there aren't consistent guardrails across providers. The canary experiment makes that problem concrete: it's systematic and it spans platforms. For any company whose employees are using cloud-based AI tools, the question isn't whether data is leaking. It's how much and where it's going.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

This is a board-level risk. If sensitive company data, client information, or proprietary strategy is flowing through AI pipelines you don't control, you have a liability exposure that most AI usage policies haven't been written to address.

Operations

Every department using cloud AI tools without explicit data governance policies is contributing to this exposure. An audit of what data is being entered into which tools, by whom, is the minimum starting point.

Engineering

Claude Ship's autonomous code review capability and the AI-driven zero-day exploit reported by Amazon are on opposite sides of the same risk. AI in your development pipeline can accelerate shipping, and it can also accelerate the attack surface against you.

What To Do This Week

  • Conduct an immediate audit of every cloud-based AI tool in use across your organization, including tools employees have adopted on their own without IT approval. For each tool, determine what data is being input, what the provider's training data policy is, and whether employees understand the exposure.
  • Check whether training data opt-outs are actually enabled on every AI platform your organization uses. Many platforms have this setting, but it's rarely on by default and even more rarely communicated clearly to end users.
  • Brief your team on the canary experiment findings specifically. When you frame it as 'AI pipelines are leaking private data at scale and we now have proof of how systematic it is,' people take the data hygiene conversation more seriously.

SHIFT 4 PREPARE This quarter Enterprise adoption

The shared knowledge base just went mainstream, and organizations without one are already behind

Akshay Kothari's guide on building an org-wide AI context system hit 6.5 million views and 24,000 bookmarks before the weekend ended. His core argument is exactly what I've been writing about for months: most companies use AI heavily but in a siloed way, with each employee feeding their own chatbot context from scratch. That blocks automation, blocks continuous learning, and means your AI tools are only as good as whatever individual employees remember to tell them each time. The viral response tells you this problem has been building under the surface and a lot of people recognized themselves in it immediately.

The fix he's describing is a shared context layer the whole organization draws from, which is the same principle behind the KAEng concept I've been developing. The idea is straightforward but hard to execute without structure: convert scattered documents, media, and institutional knowledge into structured files in a central knowledge base that AI can actually query. Right now, most organizations have knowledge distributed across drives, inboxes, Slacks, PDFs, and people's heads, with no single source of truth and no way for AI to access it reliably.

This connects to a pattern that's been building all week. On November 19th, Google expanded Gemini Tasks for background work in persistent sessions. On November 20th, Microsoft recast Copilot as the new OS for work. Both of those moves only deliver on their promise if the AI has access to rich organizational context. Without a knowledge base, you're running a powerful system on empty. The companies quietly building this foundation right now will have a compounding advantage over the ones still treating AI as a chat interface.

Who This Affects

Marketing Teams

If your campaign analytics, customer insights, and brand guidelines exist in separate silos that your AI tools can't access together, you're leaving the most valuable use cases untouched. A shared context layer is what turns AI from a writing assistant into a strategic tool.

Creative Teams

Creative teams working without centralized brand, campaign history, and performance data are rebuilding context from scratch every project. That's the exact friction a knowledge base solves, and it directly multiplies what AI can do in a brief-to-launch workflow.

Leadership/C-Suite

The 6.5 million views on Kothari's guide signal that organizational knowledge management just became a mainstream AI priority. If your competitors are building shared context layers and you're not, that gap compounds over time.

What To Do This Week

  • Map where your organization's knowledge actually lives right now: documents, past campaigns, analytics, brand assets, HR materials, and anything else teams regularly need to reference. You can't build a shared context layer until you know what you're consolidating.
  • Identify the one or two departments with the most fragmented knowledge problem and start there. For most marketing organizations, it's the gap between what analytics teams know and what creative teams are building against. Closing that specific gap first gives you a concrete proof of concept.
  • Read Kothari's guide. It's practical and free, and 6.5 million people found it worth bookmarking for a reason.

SHIFT 5 MONITOR This quarter Geopolitical risk and infrastructure consolidation

The AI infrastructure war is becoming a political and economic pressure point that every large tech buyer needs to track

The Nvidia story ended the week in a strange place: Jensen Huang is back as CEO after 11 days, the stock jumped 30% in premarket on reinstatement news but still sits 33% below its October record, Megaspeed is headed for the US export blacklist, and Huang is scheduled to testify before the Senate next week with the intelligence investigation still running. That's a live wire, not a resolution.

It's happening at the same time Trump floated a 'tremendous tax' on US companies building AI data centers abroad, which directly threatens Microsoft's $15 billion overseas commitment and similar bets by Google and Amazon. Amazon's $7.5 billion acquisition of Decart to close its chip gap with Nvidia through software signals that the chip supply chain is fragile enough that a major cloud provider is spending nearly as much on a software fix as some countries spend on defense. The Anthropic IPO completing as the biggest ever, followed immediately by the Nasdaq dropping 5% and erasing all of 2026's gains, adds a macro volatility layer that makes every infrastructure spending decision more complicated.

For most businesses, the direct impact isn't the geopolitics. It's the downstream effects on pricing, availability, and reliability of the cloud AI services you depend on. If the infrastructure war between the US and China intensifies, if the data center tax proposal moves forward, or if Nvidia's governance situation creates supply uncertainty, the cost and availability of AI compute shifts with it. This week showed that the hardware and policy layers underneath AI tools can move fast and without warning.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

Infrastructure policy risk is now a real input to AI strategy. A tax on overseas data centers or further export controls on AI chips can affect the pricing and availability of the cloud AI services you've built workflows around. This belongs in your risk assessment.

Operations

If your organization runs AI workloads on cloud infrastructure tied to Nvidia chips or specific hyperscalers, the Huang situation and Amazon's chip-gap acquisition are worth tracking. Vendor concentration risk in AI compute is real and poorly understood by most ops teams.

What To Do This Week

  • Map your organization's AI tool stack back to the underlying infrastructure providers. Knowing which tools run on which cloud providers, and how exposed those providers are to chip supply and policy risk, gives you visibility you probably don't have right now.
  • Track the Huang Senate testimony and the Megaspeed blacklist decision over the next two weeks. Those two data points will tell you whether this stays a corporate governance story or escalates into broader supply chain disruption.
  • If you're evaluating any significant AI infrastructure investments this quarter, build a scenario into your planning that accounts for data center tax policy and further export controls. It's a reason to understand your exposure, and a reason to avoid overcommitting to a single provider.
