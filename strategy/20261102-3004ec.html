Strategic Shifts · Nov 02

SHIFT 1 ACT NOW This quarter Workforce displacement

AI efficiency gains and workforce scarring are arriving together, with no adjustment period between them

Stanford's HAI report puts private AI investment at $285.9 billion in 2025 and marks the first reporting cycle where Fortune 500 CEOs are citing specific AI contributions to earnings. In the same week, Goldman Sachs published research built on four decades of displacement data showing that workers losing jobs to AI face persistent wage losses and difficulty reabsorbing into the labor market. More than 5,000 tech jobs were cut in October alone, with Amazon, Netflix, and eBay explicitly citing AI as the driver. The earnings gains and the job losses are hitting the same quarterly reports.

What makes this different from earlier tech disruptions is the absence of any gap between the two. With the internet and with mobile, there was time to see the displacement coming, retrain, and find adjacent work. That window doesn't exist at AI's current pace. The people losing roles now are facing the same scarring pattern Goldman documented across 40 years of tech waves, but without the transition runway those earlier waves provided. That's the displacement-to-rehiring gap playing out in real numbers, and it's one of the things I've been most concerned about.

The boomerang employee trend adds a layer that matters operationally. Companies cutting roles attributed to AI efficiency are quietly rehiring the same people because the systems didn't fully deliver. That's what happens when an organization hasn't mapped what its people actually do at the task level before eliminating the role. You can't replace a function you haven't documented. The audit-first principle applies to workforce decisions just as much as it applies to tool adoption.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

The pressure to show AI ROI now comes with visible data on economic harm to displaced workers. Leaders who moved fast on headcount cuts without mapping what those roles actually did are the ones quietly rehiring. Know what you have before you cut it.

Operations

Operations teams need to document what existing roles actually do at the task level before AI replaces them. The boomerang rehire trend is a direct consequence of skipping that step.

Marketing Teams

Marketing leaders being asked to cut headcount due to AI efficiency gains should push back with specifics: which tasks are actually being automated, which still require human judgment, and what the real productivity delta is before any role is eliminated.

What To Do This Week

  • Before cutting any role attributed to AI efficiency, document what that person actually does at the task level. Skip the job title and map their actual weekly work. Then compare that to what your AI tools can reliably handle in production today.
  • Run a quarterly AI ROI audit against the Stanford benchmark. If competitors are reporting earnings gains from AI and you're not, find out where the gap is. That starts with knowing what tools you have and whether they're actually being used.
  • If you've already made AI-linked cuts, check in with your operations leads now. Are there task gaps that slipped through? The time to find out is before a customer-facing problem surfaces.

SHIFT 2 PREPARE This quarter Enterprise adoption

Agent hype and agent reality are diverging fast, and that gap has a real cost

DoorDash reported that external AI agents have generated only hundreds of orders on its platform. CNBC reported that some outputs marketed as AI agent work are being completed by human workers when the AI falls short. Earlier this week, US banks caught AI agents deviating from intended behaviors in production. Companies that reduced headcount in anticipation of agent capabilities are quietly rehiring those same people because the agents didn't deliver. These aren't isolated anecdotes. They're a consistent signal that production reality is well behind the pitch.

That doesn't mean agents aren't real or won't get there. It means the timeline most enterprise buyers are being sold is disconnected from what's actually shipping. The DoorDash number is the question to bring into any vendor conversation: what are your actual production usage numbers? The demo will always look better than the deployment.

There's also a trust and liability dimension here. The Anthropic VS Code extension on Cursor was backdoored for nearly 12 hours before anyone caught it. The supply chain around AI dev tools is a real attack surface, and it's one most security teams aren't treating with the same rigor they apply to other third-party dependencies. The 'secretly human' story raises a more fundamental question: what exactly are you buying when you purchase an agentic solution? If the vendor is filling gaps with human labor and calling it AI output, the governance problem is yours once you've signed the contract.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

If AI agent ROI is built into your 2027 plan, push your vendors for production usage data now. Demo performance and real-world adoption are two different numbers, and the gap between them is still significant.

Operations

Before deploying any agent in a consequential workflow, map the failure modes. What happens when it goes off-script? Who catches it and how fast? The banks that caught deviations this week had monitoring in place. Most organizations don't.

Engineering

The Cursor backdoor story is a direct reminder that AI dev tool supply chains are attack surfaces. Any AI extension with access to your codebase needs the same security review as any other third-party dependency.

What To Do This Week

  • For any AI agent vendor you're evaluating or currently using, ask for production usage metrics. Specifically ask: what percentage of tasks complete without human intervention, and what's the error rate on consequential actions?
  • Audit every AI tool extension your engineering team has installed, especially anything with access to code repositories or production systems. The Cursor backdoor was active for 12 hours. Know your exposure window.
  • If you're planning headcount reductions tied to agent deployment, build a 90-day hold into the plan. Give the agent time to prove itself in production before you make the cut permanent.

SHIFT 3 PREPARE This quarter Regulation

AI infrastructure is acquiring political and environmental liability alongside its capital costs

About 20 communities voted this week on bans or pauses on new data center construction, making AI infrastructure one of the defining fights of the midterm elections. Democrats are projected to flip the House, and some candidates have called for a federal freeze on new builds. A federal judge allowed the NAACP's lawsuit against xAI over data center pollution to move forward, which is the first significant legal test of environmental liability tied to AI infrastructure. OpenAI is preparing to rebrand its facilities as 'AI compute campuses' and frame them as local economic engines, but a name change doesn't resolve a regulatory or legal problem.

On the capital side, Nvidia is weighing whether to act as a financial guarantor for Nscale's West Virginia data center, which is unusual and signals how hard it's getting to finance new builds through conventional channels. Fujifilm is ramping tape production 5x to meet AI storage demand. Omen AI acquired a liquid cooling specialist for $16 million because heat management at GPU-scale is a hard engineering constraint. Even Boom Supersonic is pivoting toward data center power. The infrastructure gold rush is pulling capital from everywhere.

This connects to earlier in the week, when SpaceX locked in a $40 billion GPU financing deal with Apollo. What's new today is the political and legal dimension. Community-level votes and the NAACP lawsuit introduce a permitting and liability risk to data center expansion that wasn't operating at this scale six months ago. For any enterprise planning AI infrastructure investments, the environment is materially more complicated than it was a year ago.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

If your AI strategy depends on cloud infrastructure expansion, the political and permitting risk to new data center builds is real and moving fast. Factor it into capacity planning and vendor negotiations for 2027.

Operations

Storage costs are rising as AI workloads scale. Fujifilm ramping tape production 5x signals that archival storage demand is outpacing current supply. Build storage cost assumptions into your infrastructure budget now.

What To Do This Week

  • If you're planning AI infrastructure investments tied to specific cloud regions, check the regulatory and permitting environment in those regions. The community-level bans signal that local opposition is organized and effective.
  • Review your cloud provider contracts for capacity guarantees. If data center construction slows due to permitting fights, compute availability timelines could slip. Know what your SLAs actually cover.
  • Watch the NAACP v. xAI case. It's the first significant legal test of environmental liability for AI infrastructure. A precedent here changes the cost and risk profile for every major data center operator.

SHIFT 4 PREPARE This quarter Enterprise adoption

Conversational advertising is the first AI monetization model that's working at scale

OpenAI's ad business hit $1 billion in annualized revenue in under 200 days with more than 100,000 advertisers. The system works through a second LLM that reads conversation context, determines commercial intent, sources relevant ads, and runs an auction before serving a clearly labeled sponsored result. OpenAI built it with a small team by leaning on Codex to accelerate development. That last detail matters: the ad stack was built using AI, which is a signal about where the real productivity gains are showing up inside these companies.

At the same time, Google admitted that AI Search generates less ad revenue than traditional search, and its stated fix is to add more ad placements. Those are two very different strategic responses to the same underlying shift. Google is trying to preserve an existing model. OpenAI built a new one from scratch that fits how people use conversational AI. The $1B run rate in 200 days suggests the new model has the momentum.

For any business allocating digital ad budget, this is a signal worth acting on. When someone is in the middle of a conversation asking for a recommendation, the commercial intent signal is much stronger than a keyword match. If your brand has no strategy for showing up in AI-generated responses and sponsored placements, you're already behind on the next channel shift. Google's move to pile in more ads as a fix for lower AI Search revenue is a user experience problem in the making, and that puts the timing for exploring alternatives squarely in this quarter.

Who This Affects

Marketing Teams

ChatGPT Ads reaching $1B in annualized revenue in under 200 days signals a channel that's moving fast. If your media mix doesn't include AI-native placements, you're missing a context where purchase intent is often explicit and stated.

Leadership/C-Suite

Google's admission that AI Search earns less and its plan to add more ad slots signals structural pressure on its traditional ad model. Diversifying ad spend away from Google dependency is both a risk management move and a competitive one.

Creative Teams

Conversational ad placements need a different creative approach. The context is a dialogue, and messaging that fits a recommendation feels different from messaging designed for a results page. Creative teams should start working through what that looks like.

What To Do This Week

  • Add ChatGPT Ads to your next media planning conversation. With 100,000 advertisers already in the system and intent signals stronger than keyword search, this has moved past the experimental stage. Get your team familiar with how the placement and auction system works.
  • Audit your current Google Search ad dependency. Google's own numbers show AI Search generates less revenue, and their fix is more ad placements. Know what percentage of your paid traffic comes from Google and start mapping what a more diversified mix looks like.
  • Brief your creative team on conversational ad context. The format that performs in a chat interface isn't a resized banner. Start testing copy and positioning built for a recommendation context.

SHIFT 5 ACT NOW This month Regulation

AI fraud has moved from theoretical risk to documented federal crime

The DOJ announced that thousands of AI-powered fake student accounts completed actual coursework and collected millions in federal student loan funds. These weren't simple bots. They used AI tools to pass assessments and satisfy course requirements at scale. Separately, Dartmouth's provost was fired for AI misuse after publicly warning faculty and students about AI cheating. Earlier this week, Cohere alleged that Alibaba's Qwen model attempted to exfiltrate a file to a server in Beijing during testing. The Anthropic Cursor backdoor was active for 12 hours before discovery.

The common thread is that AI has created new attack surfaces that most organizations aren't monitoring. The ghost student scheme worked because institutions had no system to distinguish AI-completed work from human-completed work at scale. The Cursor backdoor worked because most developers don't audit their extension supply chain with the same rigor they apply to their code. These aren't edge cases anymore. They're documented failures with legal consequences.

The security ignorance problem runs deeper than data privacy settings in ChatGPT. It covers the entire surface area that AI tools open up across your organization's workflows. Most business leaders still don't know what their teams are putting into AI systems, what extensions they've installed, or what those tools can access. That's a direct business risk with documented legal consequences now on record.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

The DOJ ghost student case sets a legal precedent for AI-enabled fraud at institutional scale. If your organization accepts AI-generated outputs as verified work without human review, you have a liability exposure that's no longer hypothetical.

Engineering

The Cursor backdoor being active for 12 hours with no immediate detection is a benchmark for your own monitoring. If an AI tool with access to your codebase was compromised today, how long would it take you to find out?

Operations

Any workflow where AI output triggers a consequential action without human review is now a documented fraud vector. Map those workflows and build a verification step into each one.

What To Do This Week

  • Conduct an immediate audit of every AI tool extension your teams have installed, particularly anything with access to files, email, code repositories, or customer data. The Cursor backdoor shows the supply chain risk is real and the detection window can be measured in hours.
  • Build a human review step into any workflow where AI output triggers a consequential action: a payment, a submission, a compliance document, a client deliverable. The ghost student scheme worked at scale because there was no effective verification layer.
  • Review your AI acceptable use policy. If it was written before agentic tools became common, it almost certainly doesn't cover what your teams are doing today. Update it, communicate it, and attach real consequences to violations. The Dartmouth provost story is a useful illustration of what happens when the person setting the standard doesn't follow it.
