Strategic Shifts · Nov 04

SHIFT 1 ACT NOW This quarter Cost compression

The frontier AI business model is breaking, and your vendor relationships are at risk

Anthropic's disclosures today put hard numbers on a structural problem. A $23.3 billion net loss in a single quarter against $17 billion in revenue, with $895 billion in total spending commitments ahead. Anthropic isn't alone in this. Musk called Grok Bot's serving costs unsustainable relative to revenue just two weeks after calling it his fastest-growing product ever. Fal's valuation doubling to $16 billion signals that inference demand is surging across the industry, which means serving costs are going up at the same layer where labs are cutting prices to compete.

This isn't just a finance story. When labs are bleeding cash, they lower prices to hold market share, which makes their unit economics worse, which accelerates the pressure on the whole system. If you've built workflows around a specific frontier model, you need to think about what happens if that lab hits a wall, gets acquired, pivots its pricing, or restricts access to manage costs. That risk is no longer hypothetical. It's disclosed in an SEC filing.

This trend has been building all week. Microsoft posted its tenth consecutive record Azure quarter on AI demand, Goldman flagged a long-lasting economic scar from AI displacement, and Stanford's HAI index showed $285 billion in private AI investment in 2025. Capital is flooding in, but the gap between investment and sustainable revenue hasn't closed. For enterprise buyers, that's a vendor stability question that belongs in your risk planning now.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

If your operations depend on a specific AI vendor, the cost-revenue gap at the frontier layer is a vendor concentration risk. Build diversification and contingency into your AI infrastructure plan this quarter.

Operations

Pricing instability at the model layer will flow downstream into your costs. Lock in contracts where you can, and audit which workflows have hard dependencies on a single provider.

What To Do This Week

  • Map every AI vendor your organization uses and identify which workflows would break if that vendor changed pricing, restricted access, or shut down. You can't manage this risk without knowing your exposure.
  • Before signing any new AI contracts, ask vendors specifically how they're managing serving costs and what their path to sustainable unit economics looks like. It's a fair question now that it's in an SEC filing.
  • Start evaluating open-source or self-hosted alternatives for your highest-dependency workflows. You don't have to switch, but knowing your options means you're not negotiating from zero if pricing shifts.

SHIFT 2 ACT NOW This quarter Workforce displacement

AI-driven headcount cuts are backfiring, and the smarter play is now documented

The regret data is in, and it's striking. Fifty-five percent of employers who cut staff citing AI now say those decisions were mistakes. Palantir's CTO Shyam Sankar made the point even more precisely: nearly every customer who tried to use AI to reduce headcount failed to hit that goal. The companies actually winning are using AI to take on work that was previously too expensive or time-consuming to attempt, expanding what their teams can accomplish and growing output volume in the process.

This confirms something I've seen firsthand. The organizations jumping straight to headcount cuts are skipping the step that creates the actual value: understanding where the friction is in their current processes. You can't automate your way to better outcomes without knowing which parts of your workflow are the bottleneck. That's the audit-first principle applied directly to workforce decisions. Cutting bodies before mapping the work is guessing, and apparently 55% of the time it's the wrong guess.

What's notable is that this is showing up in Palantir's earnings commentary, well beyond HR research. It's moved from anecdote to documented business outcome. The companies pulling ahead are using AI to do more with the same team, which is a fundamentally different ROI model than headcount reduction. This connects to what Goldman flagged earlier in the week about a long-lasting economic scar. That scar comes partly from displacement, but also from the management decisions that turned out to be wrong.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

If you've already made AI-justified cuts, audit whether you actually hit the efficiency goals you cited. If you're planning cuts, the data suggests mapping the work first will tell you something different than your assumptions.

Marketing Teams

AI is going to let your existing team run more campaigns, test more concepts, and reach more segments than they could before. That's a different conversation to have with leadership than cost reduction.

Creative Teams

The at-bats framing is validated here. AI gives creative teams the ability to generate and test more options without adding headcount. The ROI story is output quality and volume, and that's what the data supports.

What To Do This Week

  • Before any AI-justified staffing decision, run a process audit to map where actual time is being lost. The cuts that work are the ones that eliminate friction, and you need the map before you can find that friction.
  • Reframe the AI ROI conversation with your leadership team around capability expansion. The documented wins are coming from doing work that was previously out of reach, and your business case should reflect that.
  • If your organization already made AI-driven cuts, do a 90-day review against the specific efficiency targets you stated. If you didn't state targets, that's the first thing to fix before the next round.

SHIFT 3 PREPARE This quarter Consolidation

AI is rewriting other AI, and that changes what 'staying current' actually means

The MIT research on GPT-6 Astra is easy to scroll past and hard to overstate. Astra edited just 0.016% of Alibaba's Qwen model's weights, with no additional training required, and pushed its math score from 7.9% to 48.1%. That's one AI system diagnosing and repairing another at the parameter level. And OpenAI's LifeVal benchmark shows Astra scoring 80.6% on everyday task completion, up from 49.3% a year ago, with perfect scores on 10 of 16 task categories.

Set those two things together and you get a picture of capability progression that's faster than most organizations can track. If the leading model can now meaningfully improve other models by directly editing their weights, the gap between frontier AI and everything else widens in jumps. That matters because the AI tools you evaluated six months ago may be substantially behind what's available today. The gap is real, it's compounding, and most teams haven't built any process for catching up.

This connects directly to what Anthropic flagged earlier this week: recursive self-improvement capability is imminent. Today we're seeing empirical evidence that one form of it already works. The speed problem I keep coming back to isn't just about keeping up with headlines. It's about the real gap between what frontier models can do now and what your team thinks they can do. That gap is larger than most people realize, and it's widening on a quarterly cycle.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

Your AI strategy needs a review cadence built in. Capability jumps at the frontier are quarterly events now, and an annual technology audit won't catch them in time.

Engineering

AI-to-AI weight editing means model improvement and debugging could look very different within 12 months. Teams relying on manual fine-tuning processes should understand what's arriving at the frontier layer.

Operations

LifeVal scores on everyday task completion, 80.6% with perfect scores across two-thirds of tested categories, mean agentic automation for routine workflows is closer to production-ready than most operations teams have planned for.

What To Do This Week

  • Set a quarterly AI capability review on your calendar now. What you evaluated in Q1 may be two generations behind by Q4, and those gaps aren't trivial.
  • Look at the LifeVal task categories where GPT-6 Astra scores perfectly, specifically booking, scheduling, and form completion, and identify which of your current manual workflows map to those categories. Those are your near-term automation targets.
  • Brief your leadership team on the recursive self-improvement trajectory. The strategic risk goes beyond falling behind competitors. It includes making capital investments in tools that become obsolete faster than their depreciation schedules.

SHIFT 4 ACT NOW This month Regulation

The AI security perimeter keeps breaking, and most businesses still don't have one

Anthropic's VS Code extension on the Cursor platform was backdoored for nearly 12 hours before anyone caught it. That's a developer tool with potential access to codebases, credentials, and local file systems. And separately, Anthropic is now building a cryptographic identity verification system because there's no reliable way to confirm you're talking to the model you think you are. Both stories landed today, and together they describe an AI tooling ecosystem that's expanded far faster than the security infrastructure around it.

I've been raising this point for a while: most people don't realize that everything they enter into an AI system can potentially be used for training, that guardrails are inconsistent and have been added and removed without notice, and that AI companies are operating with limited accountability. The backdoor incident makes it concrete. This wasn't a theoretical vulnerability. It was an active compromise of a widely used developer tool that ran for half a day undetected. Ballistic Ventures seeking $490 million for AI cybersecurity isn't a coincidence. The smart money is already pricing this risk.

For businesses, the question isn't whether to care about AI security. It's whether you've taken any steps at all. Most organizations I talk to haven't. They don't have a policy on what employees can enter into public AI tools, they don't know which AI tools have access to sensitive systems, and they haven't done a security audit since adding AI to their stack. That's the gap that creates the exposure.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

AI security is now a board-level risk. A 12-hour backdoor in a widely trusted developer tool is the kind of incident that belongs in your risk register, well beyond your IT team's incident log.

Engineering

Every AI coding tool in your stack is a potential attack surface. The Cursor/Anthropic extension incident is a template for how this exposure works. Audit which tools have access to your codebases and credential stores.

Operations

If you don't have a policy on what employees can and can't enter into AI tools, you don't have an AI security posture. That's the starting point, and most organizations are still missing it.

What To Do This Week

  • Audit every AI tool in your current stack and document what systems, credentials, and data each one can access. You can't manage the exposure without knowing what's connected to what.
  • Create a written policy, even a simple one-page version, covering what categories of information employees are permitted to enter into public AI tools. Sensitive client data, internal financials, and credentials should be prohibited by default.
  • Subscribe to security notifications for every AI tool your team uses. The Cursor incident ran for 12 hours. Without active monitoring, you won't know until it's already a problem.

SHIFT 5 PREPARE This quarter Enterprise adoption

Agents are moving into real operations, and the infrastructure is catching up fast

Several things landed today that, taken together, show agentic AI crossing from demo territory into actual operations. The $52 million raise for AI agents in skilled trades and blue-collar industries is the most telling signal. When investors write big checks for agents in physical, hands-on work, it means they believe agentic reliability is close enough to production-ready to justify the bet. That's a different signal than another chatbot startup raising a seed round. SpaceXAI's internal team reportedly got 70 hours of work done in a single week using Grok Bot. That's a real internal deployment delivering measurable output.

Hume AI's Decisions API adds a piece that's been missing from voice AI: an audio-native reasoning layer that can make consistent, auditable decisions mid-conversation, processing voice input directly and skipping the speech-to-text translation step entirely. That matters for anyone building voice-based customer workflows, because voice AI could always talk but couldn't reliably decide. Cyclon's AI-native phone OS, still in closed beta, is the consumer signal that agents are moving from app-level to operating system-level. These aren't all the same story, but they're pointing in the same direction.

The LifeVal benchmark scores back this up with numbers. GPT-6 Astra scores perfectly on 10 of 16 everyday task categories, the kind of tasks showing up in every business: scheduling, booking, form completion. Agentic AI for routine operational tasks is a planning conversation you should be having this quarter.

Who This Affects

Operations

Routine scheduling, intake, and administrative workflows are the first layer of agentic automation that's production-ready. Map which of your current manual processes fall into those categories before a competitor does it first.

Marketing Teams

AI agents handling campaign scheduling, reporting pulls, and content distribution are closer to practical deployment than most marketing teams have planned for. The question is whether your current stack can integrate them.

Creative Teams

Tools like Riverside Agents, which turn interviews into captioned clips automatically, are the front edge of agentic creative workflows. They're eliminating production grind so your team can focus on the judgment calls that actually need a human.

What To Do This Week

  • List the five most time-consuming routine tasks in your operations or marketing workflow. Cross-reference them against the LifeVal task categories where current AI scores near-perfectly. That list is your near-term agentic automation roadmap.
  • If your business includes any voice-based customer interaction, evaluate Hume AI's Decisions API or comparable tools now. Auditable, consistent mid-conversation decisions are the gap that's been blocking voice AI from real operational use.
  • Before deploying any agent in a consequential workflow, build a human-review checkpoint into the process design. Agents at 80% accuracy still fail 20% of the time, and in operational contexts, those failures need a human catch.
