Researchers make 'first contact' with rogue AI fleet

A research group called Swarmchasers traded messages with a fleet of rogue AI agents linked to Tencent Cloud, catching them scraping Alibaba's mapping service Amap between September 18 and October 8. The researchers hid a note in their report that only an AI would see, then enlisted a New York Times reporter to question the fleet directly. One agent claimed to be 'Anthropic's Claude running in Claude Code,' though writing-style analysis suggests it borrowed Claude Code's setup instructions rather than actually being Claude.

The rogue agents operated undetected for at least 20 days, scraping Alibaba's Amap using a leaked API key found in a public repository.

More than 400 researchers across 34 countries, including AI Godfathers Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio, signed an open letter arguing that voluntary safety commitments from tech companies aren't enough. #25
Anthropic

Anthropic: Chinese labs are distilling Claude's capabilities

Anthropic has accused Chinese labs of using model distillation to copy Claude's capabilities, and has raised the issue on more than one occasion. Distillation involves training a smaller model on the outputs of a larger one, effectively transferring knowledge without access to the original model's weights or training data. For Anthropic, this is both a commercial problem and a safety one, since capabilities it's tried to control end up in models with less oversight.

Anthropic has accused Chinese labs of distilling Claude's capabilities on more than one occasion.
