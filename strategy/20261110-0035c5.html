Executive Summary

What today's stories show, taken together, is that the AI industry is running into real friction from multiple directions at once, and that friction is going to affect your business faster than most leaders realize. Voters just handed AI infrastructure its clearest political defeat yet: 40 of 41 ballot measures went against data center expansion, Texas now has a governor who campaigned on construction moratoriums, and Microsoft is already backing away from neocloud deals. Meanwhile, OpenAI closed only $11 billion of a $30 billion-plus funding target, and Anthropic is pricing its IPO into a market that's already sold off on AI stocks. The money is still flowing, but the cracks in the foundation story are visible now. This isn't a one-day blip. The midterm data center backlash has been building all week, and today it landed with consequences.

The Nvidia situation deserves its own sentence. Jensen Huang admitted an affair with someone US intelligence believes was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. The stock is down nearly 9% on the week and sitting $1.27 trillion below its October peak. The board opened an investigation, Huang has until November 11 to respond to Congress, and Korea is now openly worried that US export restrictions could start affecting Samsung and SK hynix. Nvidia signed a hardware partnership in Seoul the same day it refused to say whether congressional pressure might affect its Korean relationships. That's not a stable situation. If you're in a supply chain or technology stack that depends on Nvidia GPU availability, you should be thinking about what a disruption scenario looks like.

Underneath all the financial and political noise, the actual capability curve isn't slowing down. METR's independent audit found Anthropic is improving its models at 1.9 times its pre-October 2025 rate, and roughly 80% of that speedup comes from AI doing the R&D work itself. An Anthropic model just verified a quantum physics conjecture that researchers couldn't crack for decades. Adobe is wiring AI agents into Photoshop, Premiere, and Firefly via MCP, connecting those tools directly into the broader agent ecosystem. The gap between what AI can do and what most organizations are actually using it for is still widening. That's the tension your business is operating inside right now.

Strategic Shifts · Nov 10

SHIFT 1 PREPARE This quarter Regulation

AI infrastructure just hit its first real political wall

This one's been building all week, and today it crystallized. Voters backed data center regulation in 40 of 41 ballot measures in the midterms. Texas, one of the most AI-friendly states in the country, just elected a governor who ran on construction moratoriums. Meta's Dina Powell McCormick warned publicly that building data centers is going to get harder. Microsoft is already pulling back from neocloud deals worth tens of billions. And OpenAI closed only $11 billion of a $30 billion-plus funding target, while Anthropic is trying to price an IPO into a market that's already sold off on AI stocks.

This isn't just political sentiment. It's starting to show up in capital allocation decisions in real time. When Microsoft backs away from infrastructure deals and the two most valuable AI companies in the world are both struggling to close their funding rounds at their target valuations, that's a signal worth taking seriously. The 'build it and they'll pay for it' assumption that's driven AI infrastructure investment for the last two years is under real pressure now.

For business leaders, the practical implication is about timing and cost. If data center capacity gets constrained by regulation and construction slowdowns, inference costs don't come down as fast as the models have implied. The roadmap your AI vendors have been promising assumes continued infrastructure expansion. That assumption just got complicated.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

AI cost projections and vendor roadmaps are built on infrastructure expansion assumptions that are now in question. Board-level conversations about AI investment timelines need to account for potential cost volatility.

Operations

If inference costs don't compress as expected, the ROI math on AI-powered workflows changes. Any automation plan priced on current or declining compute costs needs a stress test.

What To Do This Week

  • Ask your AI vendors directly: how does data center construction slowdown affect their pricing roadmap and capacity commitments over the next 18 months?
  • Revisit any AI ROI projections that assumed compute costs would continue to fall. Build a scenario where they stay flat or rise modestly.
  • If your organization is evaluating enterprise AI contracts with multi-year pricing, lock in rates now before infrastructure constraints have time to push costs back up.

SHIFT 2 MONITOR This month Consolidation

The Nvidia situation is a supply chain risk hiding inside a headline

The Jensen Huang affair story is getting covered as personal scandal and corporate governance drama, which it is. But the strategic risk underneath it is a potential disruption to the GPU supply chain at the worst possible time. Huang's affair partner is someone US intelligence reportedly believes was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. Congress has asked for a closed-door briefing by November 11. The stock is down nearly 9% on the week and $1.27 trillion below its October peak. At AI Day Seoul, Nvidia signed a new hardware partnership with SK Telecom and Rebellions while simultaneously refusing to say whether congressional pressure might affect its Korean relationships. That's not a company in a stable position.

Korea is reading the room. Samsung and SK hynix, two of the most critical players in global semiconductor supply, are now openly worried about US export restriction spillover. Nvidia's NVLink Fusion deal with Korean chip startup Rebellions is a good sign for the relationship, but the refusal to give assurances in public tells you something about how much uncertainty Nvidia's leadership is navigating right now.

This matters for businesses that are building on Nvidia-dependent infrastructure, which is most of them. The risk isn't necessarily that GPU supply disappears tomorrow. It's that congressional pressure, export controls, or a prolonged board investigation creates enough uncertainty to slow partnerships, delay deliveries, or push alternative chip makers to accelerate. Google just announced two new specialized chips. The hardware competition was already intensifying before this week.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

If your AI strategy depends on Nvidia GPU availability through cloud providers or direct procurement, you need to understand how exposed you are to supply disruption and what alternatives exist.

Engineering

Teams building on Nvidia-specific infrastructure should start evaluating whether Google's TPUs or other specialized chips could serve as fallback options for critical workloads.

What To Do This Week

  • Map your actual dependency on Nvidia infrastructure, whether through direct procurement or through cloud providers like AWS, Azure, and Google Cloud, so you understand your exposure before anything changes.
  • Watch the November 11 congressional briefing deadline. How Nvidia and Huang respond will signal how much political risk is actually in play.
  • Start a low-urgency conversation with your cloud providers about GPU availability commitments and what their contingency hardware options look like.

SHIFT 3 PREPARE This quarter Enterprise adoption

AI agents are expanding everywhere, but Visa just showed us they still need a human in the seat

The agent rollout is accelerating fast. Meta Muse is now on Apple Watch and Android Wear OS, covering both major smartwatch platforms simultaneously. Apple is reportedly rebuilding Siri from scratch as a fully autonomous agent to compete. GitHub is using agents to fundamentally rethink how software gets built, with the explicit goal of lowering the barrier to software creation so far that the bottleneck moves from writing code to knowing what to build. DeepSeek's Harness agent is processing 92 trillion tokens per day across 1.54 million daily users. Adobe is wiring agents directly into Photoshop, Premiere, and Firefly. Agents are showing up everywhere at once.

And then Visa published a benchmark that should be required reading for anyone who's been told agents can handle purchasing autonomously. Their Agentic Commerce Benchmark found that agents struggle with the nuanced judgment calls real purchasing decisions require, and shouldn't be trusted to buy without a human in the loop. This isn't a fringe finding from a skeptic. It's Visa, running a benchmark on commerce agents, telling you they aren't ready to operate unsupervised in real transactions.

I've said this before in a different context: human-in-the-loop is how these systems are supposed to work right now. The agent hype is real, and the capability is real, but the gap between what agents can do in a demo and what they can do reliably in production is still significant. The VP of Marketing who gets sold on a fully autonomous AI agent managing budget decisions or vendor relationships is going to have a bad time. The right frame is: agents handle the execution, humans handle the judgment calls.

Who This Affects

Marketing Teams

AI agents can accelerate execution on campaigns, content distribution, and reporting, but autonomous budget decisions and vendor negotiations still need human sign-off based on current benchmark data.

Operations

Any workflow automation using agents for purchasing, procurement, or vendor management should have explicit human checkpoints built in. Visa's benchmark is a clear signal that autonomous commerce agents aren't production-ready.

Leadership/C-Suite

When vendors pitch autonomous AI agents for business operations, ask them specifically how their product performs on the Visa Agentic Commerce Benchmark. If they can't answer, that tells you something.

What To Do This Week

  • Before deploying any AI agent in a workflow that involves purchasing, contracting, or financial decisions, map every decision point and assign a human owner to each one. Don't assume the agent handles it.
  • Audit which of your current or planned AI tools are marketed as 'autonomous.' For each one, ask: what happens when it makes the wrong call, and who catches it?
  • Use Adobe's MCP integration announcement as a prompt to review whether your creative tools are already connecting to agent systems you haven't formally approved or secured.

SHIFT 4 ACT NOW This month Regulation

Security ignorance is becoming a liability you can measure

Claude Code accidentally exposed thousands of API keys and credentials across public GitHub repositories because users didn't realize their configuration files contained sensitive data and committed them to repos. That's the security ignorance problem showing up with a real consequence attached. OpenAI hiring Hacktron for offensive vulnerability research across its own systems is a signal that even the labs are taking this more seriously, but it doesn't protect your team from their own habits.

I keep coming back to this: most people using AI tools don't understand what happens to the data they put in. The Claude Code situation is a variation on that same theme. The risk isn't always that an AI company mishandles your data. Sometimes it's that your own team doesn't know which files contain secrets, and an AI tool makes it easier to accidentally publish them at scale.

This has been a consistent pattern across the week. The more deeply AI tools integrate into developer and creative workflows, the more surface area there is for accidental exposure. Claude Code is now part of version control workflows. Adobe's tools are now connecting to agent ecosystems via MCP. Meta Muse is on your wrist. The integration depth is increasing faster than most organizations' security awareness is keeping up.

Who This Affects

Engineering

Claude Code's GitHub exposure is a direct workflow risk. Any team using Claude Code needs to audit their repos for committed configuration files and add secrets-scanning to their CI pipeline immediately.

Operations

As AI tools connect to more internal systems via MCP and agent integrations, the attack surface for credential exposure grows. Standard IT security reviews need to include AI tool configurations explicitly.

Leadership/C-Suite

Security training for AI tool use isn't optional anymore. The Claude Code incident is the kind of thing that creates regulatory and legal exposure, beyond just technical embarrassment.

What To Do This Week

  • If your team uses Claude Code, run a secrets scan on your GitHub repositories today. Tools like GitGuardian or GitHub's built-in secret scanning can find exposed credentials in existing repos.
  • Add AI tool configuration files explicitly to your security onboarding checklist. Most developers know not to commit .env files; they don't yet know to treat Claude Code settings files the same way.
  • Before your team adopts any new AI tool that touches code, version control, or internal systems, make a 15-minute security review part of the adoption process rather than an afterthought.

SHIFT 5 PREPARE This quarter Specialization

The capability curve is accelerating faster than the business world realizes

METR published the first independent, high-access audit of AI R&D acceleration inside a frontier lab, and the headline finding is striking. Anthropic's internal capabilities index grew at 1.9 times its pre-October 2025 rate, with roughly 80% of that speedup coming from AI doing the R&D work itself. Compute scaling didn't drive it. The models are getting better faster because they're helping build better models. That's a feedback loop, and it's already running.

The same week, an Anthropic model independently verified a quantum physics conjecture that researchers hadn't been able to prove for decades. That's a frontier AI doing real scientific work in a domain completely outside software. Anthropic also published research on Claude using a sleep-like memory consolidation process to improve long-term retention, pointing toward systems that can handle much longer-running tasks without losing context.

This connects directly to something I think about a lot: even people on the cutting edge catch themselves underestimating what AI can do. The 1.9x acceleration finding from METR is the kind of data point that should recalibrate your assumptions about how much time you have before your current workflows are genuinely disrupted. If the capability curve is steepening because AI is now accelerating its own development, the window between 'this is impressive research' and 'this is in production software your competitors are using' is getting shorter every month.

Who This Affects

Leadership/C-Suite

If your AI strategy is built on a 2-3 year adoption timeline, the METR finding is a reason to revisit that assumption. The capability curve is steepening in a way that shortens the comfortable planning horizon.

Creative Teams

Claude's memory improvements and the broader capability acceleration mean the tools available for long-running creative projects, brand systems, and campaign development are going to look meaningfully different in 12 months.

Marketing Teams

The gap between what AI can do and what your team is using it for is almost certainly wider than you think. The METR audit is a prompt to do an honest assessment of where your current AI use actually sits on the capability curve.

What To Do This Week

  • Use the METR finding as a forcing function: schedule a half-day session with your team to honestly assess what AI can do now in your specific workflow versus what you're actually using it for. The gap is usually bigger than expected.
  • If you haven't done a formal audit of your team's AI tool usage, start there. You can't close the gap between capability and adoption if you don't know where your baseline is.
  • Watch Anthropic's memory consolidation research. As these systems get better at maintaining context over long tasks, use cases that weren't feasible six months ago, like ongoing brand management or multi-phase campaign development, start to open up.
