TypeSafe AI raises $870M at $7.5B for decision model Jev

#2
Microsoft

Satya Nadella: Treat AI like it's already compromised

Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella published an essay arguing that AI models should be treated as potential insider threats, not trusted systems. He laid out seven concrete standards: external observability logs, no model verifying its own work, tamper-proof audit trails, continuous adversarial testing, independent organizational control, built-in emergency brakes, and timely failure disclosure. The essay hit 10 million views and follows a similar piece from Microsoft AI head Mustafa Suleyman last month.

Nadella's essay proposing seven zero-trust AI governance standards reached 10 million views.

#3
Atomic machines

Atomic Machines exits stealth with $250M, 'vibe manufacturing'

Atomic Machines, led by Jeff Holden, who helped build Amazon Prime and ran Uber's self-driving program, came out of six years of stealth with $250M raised and an AI-guided factory it calls the Matter Compiler. The system builds tiny working machines, think micro-pumps, motors, and gears too small to see without a microscope, directly from a digital design with no retooling required when the code changes. Its first commercial product is PrimeSwitch, a fingertip-sized power switch for AI server farms that it says beats traditional breakers on speed and silicon on efficiency.

Atomic Machines raised $250M over six years in stealth before going public with its Matter Compiler.

#18

Why AI agents need a 'flight recorder' for safety

Analysis from The Deep View makes the case that AI agents need tamper-proof audit logs — similar to aviation black boxes — built into every meaningful action they take. The argument aligns closely with Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella's recent essay, which also called for AI actions to leave 'tamper-proof human readable evidence.' Without this kind of logging, it's essentially impossible to reconstruct how an agent reached a conclusion or triggered an outcome, which makes accountability and error correction very difficult.

Nadella's essay specifically called for every meaningful AI action to leave 'tamper-proof human readable evidence' so administrators can reproduce how models reached their conclusions.
